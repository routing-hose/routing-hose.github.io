Overview

One hose model, trained across simulators, steered by MPPI

Routing hoses and tubing requires predicting the dynamics of hollow deformable linear objects (HDLOs), which no single simulator captures reliably. R-HDLO trains one object-centric graph dynamics model on bimanual interaction data from four simulators, then infers and refines a latent mechanics code z from a few probing interactions with the hose at hand. MPPI plans with the adapted model for closed-loop routing with self-crossings, loops and many peg contacts.

On 39,559 held-out simulated hoses, the model trained on all four simulators has the lowest one-step error of every model we trained (1.72 cm vs 2.26 cm for the best single-simulator model). On 260 paired simulated starts it routes 114, against 90 for the best alternative. On a real two-arm rig it routes a 70 mm corrugated hose around three pegs in four moves.

Real robot

Routing a 70 mm hose around three pegs

A bimanual YAM rig routes a 0.83 m corrugated hose, 70 mm across, around three 45 mm pegs. The pegs are shorter than the hose is thick, so taking the hose across a peg means lifting it over. The robot probes the hose six times, fits z, then plans with MPPI and the sim-trained model. It routes all three pegs in four moves, confirmed on five settled camera observations after the grippers let go.

The model never saw a real hose in training. Six probes are enough to halve the error of assuming nothing moves, and the forecasts beat that baseline on every one of the four routing moves. They are far from exact: the routing forecasts miss by 2.4–3.6 cm on average, which is why the robot replans from the camera after every move.

Four simulators, four different hoses

The same kind of bimanual action (a grasp, three waypoints and a release) applied to hoses in simulators that model a hose in very different ways: capsule chains, particles, tetrahedral FEM. Learned dynamics

Trained on all four simulators, the most uniform model

Given the hose centreline, the pegs, a two-gripper action and z, the graph network predicts where every node comes to rest. Specialists win on their own simulator and fall apart elsewhere; the model trained on all four is the most uniform across them.

Planning

What MPPI considered, step by step

At every step MPPI samples 1,024 two-gripper action sequences, rolls each out through R-HDLO, scores the forecast hose against the routing goal, and refines the sampling distribution over a few iterations. A final check compares the weighted-mean plan, the best sample and not moving at all; the cheapest is executed and the robot replans from the observed hose.

Fine print

Notes on the videos and results

  • The real-robot result is one run. An earlier executed run on the same board (2026-09-15, before several planner fixes) made progress over 11 moves but was stopped without routing every peg. Success means every peg is on its required side on all five settled observations after release; there is no shape or tip requirement. All four executed moves came from the planner's designed goal-carry proposals; MPPI chose among their variations with the learned model. The real-rig model is the multi-simulator checkpoint with no real-world training data.
  • Real-run reconstruction. The Blender panel and the explorer draw the hose the tracker reported at 28 material points; the arms are posed by the rig's own IK from the recorded commands (grasp site within 0.5 mm of every commanded keyframe). Probe forecasts are recomputed from the run's checkpoint at the two latents it recorded, and every recomputed error matches the run's log to 0.02 mm.
  • Arms in simulation are for visualisation. Simulated grasps are virtual; the YAM arms are posed by inverse kinematics on the recorded gripper motion. An arm that cannot reach a waypoint, or would intersect the other arm, is drawn faded.
  • Simulated hose motion. Settled states are recorded by the executing simulator. During moves the hose is re-simulated where the replay matches the record within 2 mm, and linearly interpolated otherwise (always for FEM).
  • MPPI candidates in the simulation explorer come from an offline re-plan from the recorded states with the frozen model and recorded random stream; it reproduces the recorded selections on the steps shown.
  • Selected examples. Individual clips are chosen to show the method clearly; the charts report averages and counts over all trials.
  • Simulation protocol. There is no PhysX executor. Task cells G1–G4 were chosen on development seeds with the four-simulator union model; on G5–G8 ours vs AVBD only is 13 : 11 (not significant), and the AVBD-only (21 : 15) and FEM-only (16 : 6) executor splits are not significant on their own. Ours trained 160k steps, the other models 80k; at equal steps the union is 1.1% behind ours on average. The FEM executor is Newton VBD tet, on which no model was trained; a FEM success means the goal was reached before the simulator failed.
