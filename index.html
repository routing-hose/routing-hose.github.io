Abstract

Routing hoses and tubing requires predicting the dynamics of hollow deformable linear objects (HDLOs), which no single simulator captures reliably. We present R-HDLO, a framework for goal-conditioned hose routing that learns predictive dynamics across heterogeneous physics formulations and adapts them to unseen hoses through interaction. R-HDLO trains one object-centric graph dynamics model on bimanual interaction data from four simulators (Newton AVBD, an Isaac PhysX capsule chain, Newton XPBD and Genesis FEM), then infers and refines a latent mechanics code from a few probing interactions with the current hose. On 39,559 held-out simulated hoses, the model trained on all four simulators has the lowest average one-step error of every model we trained (1.72 cm node RMSE vs 2.26 cm for the best single-simulator model; 1.86 cm for a four-simulator model trained on the same total data budget as one simulator). We plug the adapted model into MPPI for closed-loop routing with self-crossings, loops and many peg contacts. On 260 paired simulated starts across 19 task cells and three executing simulators, MPPI with R-HDLO routes the hose on 114 starts, against 90 for the best alternative model.

Each simulator discretizes a hose differently (capsule chains, particles, tetrahedral FEM) and carries its own contact and bending biases. R-HDLO exports every simulator's states to the same hose centreline graph and learns a single forward model from all of them. A latent code z conditions the model on the hose at hand; it is initialised from six probing moves and refined from every observed transition. MPPI rolls the model out for 1,024 candidate two-gripper action sequences per iteration and executes the best plan, then replans.

Adapting to the Hose at Hand

Before routing, the robot makes six short probing moves. The observed responses initialise the mechanics latent z, which is then refined by gradient descent through the frozen dynamics model (80 Adam steps), and refit again after every executed action. No simulator parameters are estimated.

Planning with MPPI

At every step MPPI samples 1,024 two-gripper action sequences (horizon 2), rolls each out through R-HDLO, scores the forecast hose against the routing goal, and refines the sampling distribution over a few iterations. A final check compares the weighted-mean plan, the best sample and not moving at all; the lowest-cost one is executed, and the robot replans from the observed hose.

Notes on the Videos and Results

  • Arms are for visualization. In simulation the grasps are virtual (no arm is simulated); the YAM arms are posed by inverse kinematics on the recorded gripper motion. An arm that cannot reach a waypoint, or would intersect the other arm, is drawn faded.
  • Hose motion. Settled states are recorded by the executing simulator. During moves the hose is re-simulated by that simulator where the replay matches the record within 2 mm, and linearly interpolated otherwise (always for FEM). Where the hose wraps a peg the drawn tube can overlap the peg edge by a few millimetres.
  • MPPI candidates come from an offline re-plan from the recorded states with the frozen model and recorded random stream; it reproduces the recorded selections on the steps shown.
  • Selected examples. Individual clips are chosen to show the method clearly (often for margin); the tables report averages and counts over all trials.
  • Routing results are in simulation only. There is no PhysX executor. Task cells G1–G4 were chosen on development seeds with the four-simulator union model; on G5–G8 ours vs AVBD only is 13 : 11 (not significant), and the AVBD-only (21 : 15) and FEM-only (16 : 6) executor splits are not significant on their own. Ours trained 160k steps, the other models 80k. The FEM executor is Newton VBD tet, on which no model was trained; a FEM success means the goal was reached before the simulator failed.
